# The decision document

<!-- writing-style:disable — this file quotes banned words on purpose -->

Read this before writing a decision document. Everything in the to-ceo
SKILL.md still applies; this file adds what the long form needs.

## When one is warranted

Write one when the decision is hard to reverse, or when the analysis is
the deliverable rather than the conclusion. A reversible decision gets
made at the level closest to the work and reported as a result.

If the honest answer is three sentences, send three sentences. A
document that exists to look like diligence costs the reader minutes
and decides nothing.

## One governing thought

A single sentence answering the reader's question. Everything below it
answers "why?" or "how?" about that sentence, and nothing appears that
answers neither.

Write it before you write the document. If you cannot state it in one
sentence, you are not ready to draft — go back to the material.

## Three or four supports, no overlaps and no gaps

Each support is a claim, not a topic. If two supports make the same
point, merge them. If the reader can name a reason you did not cover,
the set is incomplete.

Order them by importance to the reader, not by the order you found
them.

## Headings that substantiate, not label

A heading carries a claim, so a reader who reads only the headings gets
the argument.

> **Before:** Three reasons to change the pool
>
> **After:** Move to a shared pool: it removes the cap, halves p95, and
> reverts in one commit

## Prose, not bullets

Complete sentences force the connective tissue a fragment hides. A
bullet can put two facts side by side without committing to how they
relate; a sentence cannot. This is the rule most worth keeping when it
feels slowest.

> **Before:**
> - Sales $37.3M, +45% MoM, +15% YoY
> - Pricing change live 3 March; last March +4%
> - Churn 3.1% to 2.4%, cause unknown
>
> **After:** Sales rose to $37.3M, 45% above the prior month and 15%
> above the same month last year. The rise tracks the pricing change
> that went live on 3 March rather than seasonality: last March grew
> 4%. Churn fell from 3.1% to 2.4% over the same window, which we did
> not predict and cannot yet explain.

Both carry the same numbers. Only the second commits to what causes
what, and to what is still unexplained.

## Opening on a problem the reader does not yet see

When the reader already wants the answer, lead with it. When they do
not yet see a problem, give one sentence of what they already accept,
then one of what changed, then the answer. Three sentences is the whole
budget. Background that is not the problem gets cut.

> **Before:** This document describes our on-call rotation. The
> rotation was set up in 2024 and covers six engineers on a weekly
> cycle, with a primary and a secondary. Handoff happens Monday at
> 10am.
>
> **After:** Six engineers have covered on-call on a weekly cycle since
> 2024, and it has held. Two left last quarter and we have not
> backfilled, so the same four now carry a week every month instead of
> every six. Move to a two-week cycle with a paid backup from the
> platform team.

## Cap the length and mean it

Six pages for a one-hour meeting, three pages for half an hour. Fill
the pages without filler rather than padding to reach them.

Anything past the cap goes in an appendix that is never required
reading. Material moves below the cap; it does not get deleted to fit.

## The document is the presentation

The long form exists because it forces the writer to think, not because
the reader enjoys length. Expect the reader to read it in the room and
then argue with it. Write for that, not for a walkthrough.

## Checks specific to this form

1. Does one sentence govern the whole document?
2. Do the supports cover the question without overlapping?
3. Does every heading carry a claim rather than name a topic?
4. Does any bullet list carry an argument that needs connective tissue?
5. Is the data in the document, rather than gestured at?
6. Is everything past the cap in an appendix rather than cut?

Sources for this file are in `source-notes.md`: Minto on the governing
thought, supports, and substantiating headings; Bryar and Carr on the
